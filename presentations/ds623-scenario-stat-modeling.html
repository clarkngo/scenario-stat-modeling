Scenario-Driven Statistical Modeling

An AI-guided framework for deriving and revising algorithmic baselines from scratch

Clark Jason Ngo — MS in Data Science, City University of Seattle
Problem

A fitting routine never tells you which assumption it broke

Learners meet estimators as finished formulas or one-line library calls. When real data violate an assumption, the routine still returns coefficients — silently wrong ones.

  • Hidden assumptions — listed once in a textbook, then forgotten
  • Silent failure — a model can fit training data while learning the wrong relationship
  • Research question — can AI-guided scenario shocks help learners derive, stress-test, and revise baselines from first principles?

Baseline · Section 2

Ordinary least squares, derived

\[ \mathcal{L}(\beta) = \tfrac{1}{2n}\lVert y - X\beta\rVert_2^2 \qquad \nabla_\beta\mathcal{L} = -\tfrac{1}{n}X^\top(y - X\beta) = 0 \]
\[ \hat\beta_{\text{OLS}} = (X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y \]
  • Convex — Hessian XᵀX/n is PSD, so the stationary point is global
  • MLE under Gaussian noise — and BLUE by Gauss–Markov
  • Influence ψ(r) = r — unbounded: breakdown point 0
Scenario shock · Sections 3–4

A unit-entry error records prices ×10

During a listing-system migration, a share of training prices is entered ten times too large. The test set stays clean.

  • Targets — the light-tailed Gaussian error assumption
  • Data — synthetic, known β = (300, 85, −20), σ = 40
  • Protocol — 200 replications per shock level
Revision · Section 5

Keep the line, change the loss

\[ \rho_\delta(u)=\begin{cases}\tfrac12u^2 & |u|\le\delta\\ \delta|u|-\tfrac12\delta^2 & |u|>\delta\end{cases} \qquad \psi_\delta(u)=\operatorname{clip}(u,-\delta,\delta) \]
\[ w_i=\min\!\left(1,\tfrac{\delta}{|u_i|}\right) \qquad \hat\beta^{(t+1)}=(X^\top W X)^{-1}X^\top W y \]
  • Bounded influence — no single row can move β̂ more than δ allows
  • δ = 1.345 — ≈95% of OLS efficiency when errors really are Gaussian
  • IRLS — solve a weighted LS on √w-scaled rows until β̂ stops moving

Discussion

The shock exposed an assumption, not a bug

  • Targeted revision — linear structure kept; only the loss changed
  • Why Huber degrades at 30% — bounded influences still add up when every outlier pushes upward
  • Next — Tukey bisquare, least trimmed squares, a real housing dataset, more scenarios
Lessons learned

The loss function is the model's opinion about errors

Derive before you import. Evaluate on clean data after training on shocked data. Revise the assumption that broke, not the whole model.
